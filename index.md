## About Me

Hi! I am a Ph.D. candidate in the [Biophysics graduate program](https://biophysics.fas.harvard.edu/) at Harvard University, advised by Professor [Cengiz Pehlevan](https://pehlevan.seas.harvard.edu/). I am fortunate to be supported by the [Kempner](https://kempnerinstitute.harvard.edu/) Graduate Fellowship.

I am interested in bridging the gap between biological and artificial neural networks, with a focus on extracting interpretable insights about recurrent computations in these systems. To do this, I draw on perspectives from machine learning and dynamical systems theory.

Here are a few questions I've been circling:

- How can we train networks to [find genuinely unique solutions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21504) to tasks---solutions that may be superior to those favored by the standard inductive biases of gradient-based optimization?
- What are the inductive biases of learning in biological and artificial systems, and when might they be at odds? (for instance, neural activity is metabolically costly, but there’s no analogous a priori reason for ANNs to avoid large activation norms)
- When, and to what extent, do [data-constrained](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/7caf9d251b546bc78078b35b4a6f3b7e-Abstract-Conference.html) or task-trained neural networks recapitulate aspects of neural representations in the brain?
